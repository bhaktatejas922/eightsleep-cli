# Blood-Sleep Connections Skill

Use when interpreting blood work in the context of sleep data, or when recommending interventions that span both domains.

## The Core Cascade

Poor sleep timing (not just duration) drives a cascade of blood marker abnormalities:

```
Late/inconsistent bedtime
  → Compressed deep sleep window
    → Reduced GH pulsatility
      → Low DHEA-S (pregnenolone stolen by cortisol)
      → Suboptimal testosterone
    → Cortisol doesn't clear during early night SWS
      → Elevated morning cortisol
        → Hepatic glucose overproduction (dawn phenomenon)
        → Fasting glucose elevated despite good insulin sensitivity
        → WBC redistribution (borderline low in peripheral blood)
  → Missed morning sun
    → Low vitamin D
      → Impaired T4→T3 thyroid conversion
      → Reduced VDR-mediated immune cell proliferation
      → Further testosterone suppression
```

## Marker-by-Marker Sleep Connections

### DHEA-S
- Produced during deep sleep via adrenal response to GH pulses
- Low DHEA-S + high cortisol = classic sleep-debt/stress pattern
- At age 28, DHEA-S should be 300-450 mcg/dL (peak years)
- Below 150 at age 28 strongly suggests chronic sleep deficit or HPA axis dysregulation
- **Paper:** Van Cauter et al. 2000 (JAMA): SWS is the primary determinant of GH secretion, and GH drives DHEA-S recovery

### Testosterone
- Leproult & Van Cauter 2011 (JAMA): 1 week of 5h sleep → 10-15% T reduction in young men
- T is produced in sleep-dependent pulses, peaking at 7-8am
- Blood drawn after 10am shows 20-35% lower T than 8am draw (diurnal variation)
- For an 11:48am draw, multiply by 1.2-1.25 for morning-equivalent estimate
- Low vitamin D causally linked to lower T (Mendelian randomization, JCEM)

### Fasting Glucose
- Spiegel et al. 1999 (Lancet): Sleep debt reduced glucose clearance 40%
- Tasali et al. 2008 (PNAS): Deep sleep suppression alone reduced insulin sensitivity 25%
- Glucose 95-99 with low TG/HDL ratio (<1.0) = dawn phenomenon, not insulin resistance
- The distinction matters: dawn phenomenon responds to sleep timing + evening walks + berberine. Insulin resistance responds to weight loss + metformin + exercise
- **CGM test:** If daytime glucose is 75-85 but fasting spikes to 95-99, it's cortisol-driven

### Cortisol
- Drawn at different times means different things
  - 8am: ref 4-22 mcg/dL (peak)
  - Noon: should be 30-40% below 8am peak
  - 4pm: ref 3-17 mcg/dL
- High noon cortisol + low DHEA-S = adrenal prioritizing cortisol over DHEA pathway
- Buxton et al. 2010 (Diabetes): 5h sleep for 7 nights raised salivary cortisol 51%
- Best assessed via 4-point salivary cortisol (morning, noon, afternoon, night)

### WBC / Immune Function
- Irwin et al. 1994 (Psychosomatic Medicine): Partial sleep deprivation reduced NK cell activity by 28%
- Prather et al. 2015 (SLEEP): Sleeping <6h → 4.2x risk of catching a cold
- Cortisol redistributes WBC from peripheral blood to tissues, causing lab-apparent leukopenia
- Vitamin D deficiency impairs VDR-mediated T-cell proliferation
- Constitutional leukopenia (South Asian, African, Mediterranean descent) compounds the picture

### Vitamin D
- Gao et al. 2018 (Nutrients): Vitamin D deficiency → OR 1.50 for sleep disorders
- Late sleepers miss morning UVB, creating a reinforcing cycle
- VDR expressed in sleep-regulatory brain nuclei (hypothalamus, brainstem)
- Target 50-65 ng/mL for optimization (not just >30 "sufficient")
- 5,000 IU D3 + 200mcg K2 (MK-7) daily. Retest at 12 weeks

### Ferritin
- Acute phase reactant; can be elevated by poor sleep-driven inflammation
- But if hs-CRP is low (<1.0), ferritin elevation is not inflammatory
- Longevity-optimized range: 50-150 ng/mL (UK Biobank genetics data)
- Blood donation drops ferritin 30-50 per donation but temporarily impairs performance
- Deprioritize if testosterone is low (iron depletion suppresses steroidogenesis)

### ApoB / Lipids
- Sleep deprivation increases hepatic VLDL production → higher ApoB
- But the effect size is small compared to genetics and diet
- Longevity targets (Attia): ApoB <60, LDL <70
- Rosuvastatin 5mg is the standard pharmacological intervention

### Creatinine / eGFR
- Fasting specimens concentrate blood → artificially elevated creatinine
- Albumin at top of range confirms hemoconcentration
- Muscle mass inflates creatinine independent of kidney function
- Always order cystatin C to disambiguate in muscular individuals
- eGFR <90 on cystatin C at age 28 warrants nephrology referral

## Retest Protocol

When ordering follow-up labs after sleep optimization:

1. **Draw at 7-8am** (testosterone peaks, cortisol is at defined reference point)
2. **Well-hydrated** (16oz water upon waking, no fasting-induced dehydration)
3. **After 2+ weeks of consistent sleep schedule** (give the system time to stabilize)
4. **Add these to the standard panel:**
   - Cystatin C (kidney disambiguation)
   - Fasting insulin + HOMA-IR (glucose context)
   - Free T3, Free T4, Reverse T3 (thyroid detail)
   - TPO + Thyroglobulin antibodies (Hashimoto's screen)
   - Lp(a), Homocysteine (CVD risk, test once)
   - LH, FSH, Prolactin (testosterone context)
   - IGF-1 (GH proxy, correlates with deep sleep)

## When to Reference This Skill

- Interpreting blood work alongside Eight Sleep data
- Explaining why a blood marker is abnormal in the context of sleep patterns
- Recommending interventions that address both sleep and blood markers
- Planning follow-up lab orders after sleep optimization
